### S25-01 failure scenario (S1)

1. A planned subtask's artifact exists and its last worker reports the acceptance checks observed, but the delivered behavior is wrong.
2. Stage 3 asks for a narrative report from the task document and checklist, not the whole-unit diff inspection and hard verification run-verify requires; it writes PASS on the observed-check claim.
3. The master renames/ticks and commits done. The driver at the same state enters runWrapup's independent verification and can write gaps and dispatch fixes instead.

### S25-02 failure scenario (S2)

1. S01 completes: run-as-subtasks renames `S01/todo.md` to `done.md` and commits; interruption strikes before S02.
2. The person invokes resume-subtasks with `S01/done.md` and `S02/todo.md`, exactly the legal state its own loop describes.
3. Its decomposition gate demands every item's `todo.md`, fails on S01 and stops; run-as-subtasks then refuses the begun task, leaving no documented automated recovery.

### S25-03 failure scenario (S2)

1. Fix round 1's first session hands over and the master lands `T-001 fix 1.1`.
2. The continuation ends; one landed fix share exists, so the manual rule supplies session count 1 rather than highest+1 = 2.
3. The second commit repeats `fix 1.1`; a fresh round interpreted literally starts at `fix 1.0`, and a restarted master cannot preserve 0148's unique per-session history.

### S25-04 failure scenario (S2)

1. Verification writes two freeform gap entries, as run-verify authorizes, but no `### Gap N —` heading.
2. The master must compare this with the next round's freeform entries using only the ambiguous word "count"; no shape gate rejects either list.
3. Two rounds that merely reword or split the same defects can be judged differently by different sessions, so the person gets an early stall stop or an unbounded loop instead of 0146's deterministic result.

### S25-05 failure scenario (S2)

1. Formulation derives eight independent cleanroom audit dimensions under the charter's precedence rule.
2. Following verify-plan-audit, it assigns each dimension to `audit.md`; following the phase declaration, it would assign eight `findings.md` files and one consolidation.
3. The wrong plan either fails the seeded obligation review or lands concurrent/multiple writers of one phase document, destroying the per-dimension ownership and evidence separation.

### S25-06 failure scenario (S2)

1. The declared divergence command writes a reference anchor and exact score to its red log, information the driver-side monitor may lawfully hold.
2. `writeLaneReceipt` (or the manual master) copies the bounded tail verbatim into `docs/R-NN/regression/`; the synthetic probe demonstrates the byte flow.
3. A later cleanroom audit/fix session reads the committed receipt and consumes the forbidden reference detail, while the source-free charter appears to have been satisfied.

### S25-07 failure scenario (S2)

1. P01 closes normally: its phases.md line becomes `- [x]`, not a `(closed: …)` suffix; P02 remains `- [ ]` with a planned pending task.
2. run-as-subtasks or resume-subtasks selects P01 because it checks only the suffix.
3. It finds no pending P01 work and stops (or reconstructs against the wrong phase), never dispatching P02's ready task.

## Design notes

The bundle policy is unusually thorough and the main run.md channel has absorbed the recent rulings well. The worst structural choice is the separately aging subtask companion pair: its stated byte-for-byte driver equivalence is belied by pre-0083 close-out and pre-current phase/resume rules. The literal ratchet is healthy as far as it goes, but its presence-only oracle gives false confidence about lifecycle semantics. Cleanroom's best choice is defense-in-depth as declared data; its weakest is assuming source-free evidence at a generic raw-log seam. Fix S25-01/S25-02 first, then the fix-loop vocabulary; S25-06 needs a structural trust-boundary ruling, not a lane-name special case.
