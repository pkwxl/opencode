## Design notes

- Health: fork/remove-fork and land are careful two-pass, refusal-first code with sound recovery shapes; their defects are edges (S20-06–09, S20-11). The permission and wall half is the weak area: it reasons about opencode's permission system from first principles and never tests against it.
- Worst choice: the wall's closed predicate is a re-implementation of opencode's matcher reduced to one key shape (`<path>/**`) plus one door name. Each blind spot (S20-01/02/03/13) comes from that reduction. 0122's own measurement (relative reads through a symlink cost nothing) already says the door, not the allow, is the primary admission surface.
- Best choice: everything derives from disk and git with no registry (0132 D6); the record parses back from the wall commit, and forks inherit their wall for free.
- Cross-ruling drift: 0138 moved opencode.json into git, and 0122/0132's "untracked" reasoning was never revisited (S20-10). 0147's boundary broke 0132/0155's recovery (S20-01). Neither ruling's Category scope enumerated wall.ts.
- Adapter neutrality: the boundary and the wall are opencode-only, and nothing says so (S20-04). This is a P14 gap that a capability flag could make loud.
- Change first: S20-05 (receipt provenance, with S11-01/06), then a W-wall wave whose first act is an opencode-matcher oracle in the tests (S20-13). With that oracle in place, S20-01/02/03 become test-visible.
