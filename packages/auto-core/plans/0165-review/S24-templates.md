### S2 failure scenarios

- **S24-01:** A work session writes its audit or self-summary to the otherwise unreserved `docs/T-NNN/report.md` with `Result: PASS`, or renames its own todo to done. The unified commit lands that state. S11-01's reproduced verifier then reads the stale PASS over its own FAIL; S12-01's reproduced reload reads done over the blocked runtime state. The template gap is not a substitute for those code fixes, but 0120 D3's assumption that programmatic copy needs no reservation is no longer tenable.
- **S24-02:** A diagnosis session includes `Choice: A` in its valid free-text Analysis. The driver rewrites the plan into a blockage document whose Decision section remains unanswered, then `plan` consumes the Analysis line as the person's choice and executes the edit. S12-02 already reproduced this end to end; the prompt currently supplies no contrary ownership instruction.

## Design notes

- The template layer is generally healthy: strict output grammars, data fencing and 154 fast rendering tests give good mechanical coverage.
- Best choice: one shared partial family for protocol copy, with role-specific constraints close to the work they govern.
- Worst choice: ownership by implication in `doc-layout` and planner duties; S11/S12 show that ambiguous copy becomes dangerous when the driver trusts session-writable verdict/state files.
- Fix S24-01 with S11-01/S12-01 and S24-02 with S12-02 first; then make the three lifecycle-copy edits with golden/ratchet updates.
- Semantic lifecycle drift survives because tests pin literals and rendering, not agreement between related templates; small cross-template contract assertions would cover the gap without judging prose quality.
