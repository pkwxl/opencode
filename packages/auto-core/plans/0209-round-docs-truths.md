## D1 — the proposal body keeps its sub-headings; a `##` heading that strands proposals is named (0165-016)

0115 D2 ruled the proposal body "free markdown, read to the next `###`/`##` heading"; 0158 D3 widened the terminator to every heading-shaped `#{3,6}` line to hold the near-miss line (a `#### R-06 notes` drafted after a released `### R-05` never rides into R-05's installed brief). The widening guarded one shape by silently eating the most natural brief shape — the sectioned proposal the stub's own hint invites ("goal, acceptance criteria, release criteria — the content of docs/R-NN/round.md", whose own stub is three `##` sections). The grammar is restated structurally, on what a line *names* rather than how many hashes it carries:

- **a round-shaped head terminates** — a level-3..6 heading naming a round id (`#### R-05`, `### R-06 x`) that is not an exact `### R-NN` head settles the current subsection and stops collection until the next exact head: 0158 D3's discipline kept whole, now scoped to the heads that could be mistaken for a proposal. An exact head still starts the next subsection.
- **every other sub-heading is body** — `#### Acceptance criteria`, `#### Notes`, any `#{3,6}` line naming no round rides verbatim into the installed brief (0115 D2's free markdown restored). The false "holds no content" problem for a proposal opening with a sub-heading dies with the truncation — its body is non-empty.
- **a `##`/`#` heading still ends the section** — but exact `### R-NN` heads after the section's end are a **named release problem** (`analysisProblems`, capped at three + count like the family's other namings) teaching the move (each proposal above the heading that ends the section, or that heading demoted below `##` — with sub-headings now body, a demoted heading is absorbable), and the round's open names the ones drafting its round: `roundBriefNearMissNote` gains the second arm, the same ℹ teaching line at the same bounded moment (the brief still the stub), so the living-governance flow — a `##`-stranded proposal drafted into a released document later, the release scan never re-running — meets the same one-line teaching the near-miss shape meets. The near-miss-only line stays byte-identical.
- **the teaching surfaces learn the levels**: the analysis stub's `## Round briefs` hint and the AGENTS.md block's analysis guidance state that sub-headings inside a proposal are its body and a `##` heading ends the section.

The alternative — keeping 0158 D3's grammar and teaching against it — is declined: the stub's own hint had already taught the sectioned shape, the person's channel (0115: the released words install verbatim) is the authority the extractor serves, and a round-shaped head is the one shape the near-miss discipline ever measured. The installed-brief comment's "structured as they structured them" (round-brief.ts) is now true of the extract too.
