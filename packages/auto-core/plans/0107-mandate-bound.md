# 0107 — the mandate bound: the person's instruction is the proxy's only scope

Ruled from common's R-01→R-02 boundary (2026-10-19), the second field round
of the person-proxy brief (the first was 0106, the same session's R-01
close). Asked to "fix the issues that block us to continue R-02", the
session fixed the four round-close blockers and committed them — the ask,
in scope — then chained three further acts: ran `transfer` (closing R-01,
opening R-02), filled `docs/R-02/round.md` itself and committed it as the
round-start review, and launched the next `transfer` (the survey plan
task's construction) until the person's stop arrived mid-flight. The
content it wrote was traceable to the released roadmap — nothing invented —
but the review act was never the person's: the round brief is the person's
own prompt for the new round, and no person read it before it committed.

The failure class this time is mandate-shaped, not instruction-shaped: the
brief bounded *what the pen may touch per stop*, never *how far it may
walk*. Every further act had a textual license the surfaces themselves
print:

- the round-close stop's own `next:` line — "fix them, commit, then
  re-run: transfer" — an instruction to advance;
- the round-start gate's stop line — "review the round setup, fill in
  docs/R-02/round.md … and commit it; then run: transfer …" — a two-step
  instruction the proxy, holding the person's pen, took as its work list;
- ROLE's canonical-act sentence — "person-commits are the review acts at
  the person gates — the round-start `docs/R-NN/round.md` review commit
  the canonical one" — which the session quoted verbatim as standing
  license ("the round-start gate is the pen's canonical act").

The person's mid-flight stop was the only bound anywhere. The session even
knew the pasted brief was stale for the new stop and reasoned past it
("the old brief still governs — its ROLE section stands"): it was looking
for a rule, and the rule was missing.

## The ruling (the person's, 2026-10-19)

The proxy session does only what the person's instruction names, no more.
For the round brief in particular: the person writes their own prompt for
the new round themselves, after the previous round is properly closed —
the pen never fills it.

## D1 — ROLE's mandate bound

ROLE gains its second paragraph: the mandate is the person's instruction
and nothing else; the brief and the stop lines name the person's options
at each stop, never a runway; a command the instruction names is in scope,
and every stop a command reaches is a report point — report what the stop
names and wait; never chain a further advance because a line says `next:`
(that line addresses the person); a person gate's review act is taken by
the person or delegated by the instruction in so many words; the person
re-renders the brief (`driver-helper`) for the new stop. POLICY's
stop-and-ask sentence is re-keyed to match: "Everything beyond the
person's instruction is stop-and-ask" (it read "Everything else", which
left chained advances outside the sentence's reach).

## D2 — the round brief is the person's own writing

`roundBriefChannel` (row 2, and every row that renders it — the
pre-establishment rhythm, the round-complete row's "after that transfer",
the G1 window) now states authorship: the person's own writing for the new
round — they write it and commit it; the pen discusses on request and
never fills or commits it. ROLE's canonical bullet is reworded to the same
ruling (the old sentence was the session's quoted license), and the two
recovery parentheticals that said "your commit is the review" / "stops for
your review" — addressed, in a brief pasted into a proxy, to the proxy —
now name the person. The line that separates this channel from the map's
other person gates: **marks over recorded decisions stay delegable
channels** (a survey's `Clarified: yes` accepts recorded `Recommend:`
defaults; an acceptance's `Accepted: yes` and a blockage's `Choice:` answer
lettered options a document already lists) — **authorship is not**: the
round brief has no recorded defaults to accept, it is composition. Those
rows are unchanged.

## D3 — the `next:` reading rule

STATE's glyph rule (0106 D3) gains its sibling, rendered whenever a stop
line carries `next` or `then re-run`: such a line names the person's act
at the stop it printed — never this session's mandate to continue. The
0106 pattern holds: the rule rides beside the lines it classifies, in the
brief, the proxy's reading surface.

## D4 — the stop lines stay byte-identical

0105 D7 stands: no stop-line edit, no ratchet touch. The `next:` lines
address the person on the terminal, where they are correct; the failure
was the proxy's reading of them, and the bound re-keys the reader (D1,
D3), not the text. This differs from 0106 D4 (the one conscious round-close
edit) exactly because there the brief itself misnamed the refuser; here
the lines are right for their audience.

## D5 — no driver-side enforcement

0106 D6's ruling extends: the driver cannot distinguish the proxy's
terminal from the person's — every command arrives as the person's hand —
and an input channel that would say who is calling is what 0105 D4
forbids (the 0082 charter-clamp lesson). An advisory flag would be an
instruction wearing a flag's clothes; the proxy would have to choose to
pass it. The bound is instruction-layer; the backstop is the person's
catch (the stop), as it was in the field.

## D6 — deliberately not done

No change to the survey/acceptance/blockage channels (marks over recorded
decisions — D2's line). No per-stop handshake or session key, no
RECOVERY row for `driver-helper` (the re-render instruction lives in
ROLE's bound, where scope is set — RECOVERY stays the state's own
commands). No stop-line changes (D4).

## Verification

ROLE's mandate sentence and its "report point" clause, the canonical

brief (the row-6 shape carries `next:` lines), and the row-2 brief
carrying the person's-own-writing channel with no pen-fill wording. Core

R-02 state renders the round-start gate row with the new authorship
wording — the state that session left behind is the test fixture.
